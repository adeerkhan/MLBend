ISSUE-001 fixed logged 2026-10-27 22:17 UTC

the digits denoiser learned nothing (ours, high)

What. examples/diffusion_digits/main.bend trained a 3-layer SiLU MLP for 3000 full-batch AdamW steps and ended at MSE 1.0258 (NumPy twin 1.0746). The target is unit-variance noise, so predicting nothing scores exactly 1.0: both arms were at or worse than the mean.

Why. value(i, j, seed) returned k*0.1 - 0.2 for k ∈ 0..6, i.e. a uniform init on [−0.2, +0.5] with mean +0.100. The first layer is 72 wide, so the layer adds 72 × 0.100 × 0.329 ≈ +2.4 to every pre-activation (measured: z1 mean +2.36, rms 2.43); SiLU is roughly linear there (h1 mean +2.16), and each following layer multiplies that common mode by fan_in × 0.100 = 6.4 — z2 +13.8, the output +88.4 rms. Against a target of rms 1.0 that is the measured first loss, 8062.6895 (the reference's is 0.963). Adam then had to undo a 10⁴× scale with lr 0.01, and 3000 steps were not enough — it landed on the mean predictor and stayed.

How it showed up. The loss dropped 8062.7 → ~18 by step 98 and then sat between 0.99 and 1.06 for the remaining 2900 steps. Downstream, the sampled cloud was meaningless: sample_mean 1.2e17, sample_std 1.075e19, 0 of 4096 pixels inside [0,1].

Component. Ours: examples/diffusion_digits/main.bend, the weight initialiser. Not BendTorch, not the schedule — the NumPy twin plateaued at the same 1.0.

Verified by. Three independent measurements: 1. The failure itself: results/diffusion-digits/run.json, and the twin's numpy_answer(). 2. A controlled ablation of our exact MLP, optimizer and data (reference/digits_ablation.py), one factor at a time — the init was the only factor that mattered:

bend [-0.2, 0.5][0,1]bend3480.0362.201.3766
torch zero-mean[0,1]bend1.01380.32110.1455
torch zero-mean[−1,1]bend1.01330.36370.1755

Fix. value() is now a 97-value hash on [−1, 1) with zero mean, scaled by 1/sqrt(fan_in) at the row builder — PyTorch's nn.Linear init made deterministic, which is what the reference uses. The NumPy twin mirrors it.

ISSUE-002 logged 2026-10-27 22:17 UTC

the two "linear schedules" are not the same schedule (ours, medium) — DECIDED (documented)

What. mlbend-generative's lin_beta_at(t, 64) is 1e-4 + 0.2799·t/64: β = 0.0044734 … 0.28, ᾱ₆₄ = 4.085385e-05. The reference's linear_beta_schedule(64) is linspace(1000/T·1e-4, 1000/T·0.02, T): β = 0.0015625 … 0.3125, ᾱ₆₄ = 1.223548e-05. Worst per-step relative gap: 65.07%.

Why. Two conventions both called "the linear schedule": DDPM's 1000-step schedule rescaled by 1000/T, and a directly parameterised 0 → 0.28 ramp. Neither is wrong; they are not interchangeable.

How it showed up. Only when the two were printed side by side (reference/lucidrains_compare.py, "the schedules"). No gate could see it, because each arm was compared with its own mirror.

Component. Ours (mlbend-generative); the reference defines the other convention.

Verified by. The script above, and the library's own diffusion.alphas_cumprod[-1] printed in the same run.

Status. Decided: documented, not changed. The ablation says it does not explain ISSUE-001 (0.1755 vs 0.1455), and the 3,000-step runs reach the same order either way. Our schedule is the 1e-4 + 0.2799·t/T ramp, which is what mlbend-generative describes in lin_beta_at; the reference's is the 1000-step schedule rescaled by 1000/T. They are different schedules, not two spellings of one, and the difference is now written down in NOTES (2026-10-28) rather than left as an unexplained divergence.

ISSUE-005 fixed logged 2026-10-27 22:17 UTC

the sampler has no x̂₀ clamp, so the cloud explodes (ours, high)

What. After ISSUE-001 the digits model learned (final loss 0.0417, better than the reference's 0.078) but its sampled cloud was std 35.8 with mean −1.39, against the data's mean 0.3076 / std 0.3767 — and 0 of 4096 pixels inside [0,1].

Why. The reverse step formed its mean directly from the raw ε̂. The vendored reference reconstructs the start and clamps it to the data range before forming the posterior mean (p_mean_variance with clip_denoised=True, and ddim_sample with clip_x_start=True, re-deriving ε̂ from the clamped start). Without that clamp one bad prediction compounds over 64 steps; with it the sample cannot leave the data range.

How it showed up. The training loss was healthy while the samples were not, which is what pointed away from the trainer and at the sampler.

Component. Ours: examples/diffusion_digits/main.bend (and its twin).

Verified by. A controlled probe with one trained MLP, sampling it four ways (reference/digits_sample_probe.py, same weights, same schedule, same seeds):

(data: mean 0.3076, std 0.3767)

Fix. Both steps now reconstruct x0 = (x − √(1−ᾱ)·ε̂)/√ᾱ, clamp it to [0,1], and form the mean from it; DDIM re-derives ε̂ from the clamped start (the reference's rederive_pred_noise). The twin mirrors both.

ISSUE-006 fixed logged 2026-10-27 22:17 UTC

both NumPy twins took the SiLU derivative at the wrong point (ours, high)

What. The two NumPy twins (reference/test_diffusion_digits.py, reference/test_diffusion.py) computed the SiLU backward as sigmoid(h) * (1 + h*(1 - sigmoid(h))) where h is the activated output. BendTorch's silu_d(dy, x) takes x = the pre-activation; the derivative is s(z)*(1 + z*(1-s(z))), s = sigmoid(z). The twins kept only h = silu(z), so their gradient was wrong wherever z ≠ silu(z).

Why. The forward was written first (h = silu(z)), and the backward was then written against h, which is the value in scope — the pre-activation was never stored.

How it showed up. It could not show up until ISSUE-001 was fixed: while both arms sat at the mean predictor the gradients are ~0, so a wrong gradient and a right one agree. After the init fix, from an identical first loss (1.0038943 vs 1.003894329, delta 6e-9) Bend reached 0.044 in 3000 steps and the twin stayed at 1.031; its sampled cloud (0.5018/0.3982) only looked sane because of the ISSUE-005 clamp.

Component. Ours: the two twins' training loops. Not Bend, not BendTorch — Bend's layer was right, which is exactly why the arms diverged.

Verified by. The identical first loss with divergent finals (above); the same bug in both twins, found by reading the backward after the divergence; and BendTorch's own silu_d in .bend/env/.../main.bend, which takes the pre-activation as x.

Fix. Both twins now keep z1, z2 and evaluate the derivative there. The gated first loss is unchanged (one step, no update), which is why this was invisible to make check.

  • H3 — the data range ([0,1] vs [−1,1]) is the cause. Dismissed by the ablation: reference init on [0,1] scored 0.1455, on [−1,1] 0.1755 — the same order, and worse centred. Kept as a fidelity option only.
  • H4 — the time embedding as 8 concatenated input features is the cause. Not supported: the ablation kept that layout and learned fine once the init was fixed. The reference injects the embedding as per-block scale/shift; a fidelity question for the model, not the cause of the failure.
  • Nondeterminism of Bend training. Two runs of the same binary (before these fixes) were bit-identical: final loss 1.0258427, same sample line. The old report card's different numbers came from a pre-2f2631b code state.
ISSUE-007 upstream logged 2026-10-27 23:55 UTC

bend file.bend overflows its stack on a 32k-element list (bendlang/bend, high)

What. The plain run path dies with bend: memory fault (machine stack overflow?), exit 1, no source location, on a twelve-line program:

import Base
import mlbend-generative@0.1.1.0/main.bend as G

def main() -> IO(Unit):
  xs = G.noise_of(7n, 32768n)
  do IO<Unit>:
    IO.print(Nat.show(List.length(&2, F32, xs)))
    return Unit{}

Where the line is. 4096, 8192 and 16384 run and print their count; 32768 and 65536 crash. Same on Bend 2.0.38 and 2.0.39. The same file built with bend … -o prog and then run prints 65536 on both versions.

Why. The run path's stack is fixed, and each list element is built by a Box–Muller call whose frame is large enough that ~32k of them do not fit; the native build has no such limit (the guide: the compiled model has no C stack; each def is a segment of a flat state machine). It is frame size, not depth alone: a 65536-element Nat list summed by recursion on the same path is fine.

How it showed up. Running examples/diffusion_digits/main.bend the documented way. --check-only passes and -o runs the full 3000-step training, so it looked like a fault in the program; it is not — it is the run path, and the message names no file and no line.

Component. bendlang/bend, the bend <file.bend> (check, then run) path.

Verified by. The program above on both versions, the 16384/32768 threshold, the -o contrast at 65536, and the Nat-list contrast. Filed as bendlang/bend#1894.

ISSUE-010 fixed logged 2026-10-28 19:50 UTC

the Bend ViT's forward disagrees with its twin at O(1) (ours, high)

What. With identical weights (Bend's own Layer.dump fed to the twin), the frozen forward at t = 64 gave Bend eps[0] = 0.106497 and the twin 0.031502; the element-wise gap was maxabs 0.343, 1.41 relative. Found and fixed on 2026-10-28, 20:25.

How it was localized. One probe printed the ladder with the dumped weights, and the twin printed the same ladder:

pointBendtwinverdict
staging row 0 (20 features)1.5151465 -0.47618893 ...sameequal
emb0 (64)-0.4354682 -0.38745803 ...sameequal
ln0 (block 1 norm, 64)0.040882647 -0.039651513 ...sameequal
att0 (block 1 attention, 64)0.07157408 -0.09983167 ...sameequal
blk0 (block 1 out, 64)-0.06204252 -0.111143276 ...-0.43706578 ...differ

So the staging, the weights, the load order, the first linear, the norm, the attention's scores, the causal mask and the output projection were all already right, and the disagreement began in the block's *residual*.

Why. AttBlk was written Seq(LayerNorm, Residual(Att)). Seq applies left then right, so that computes

ln(x) + att(ln(x))

— the residual is taken on the normalized value. A pre-LN transformer block is

x + att(ln(x))

and the twin implemented that. Both are correct for their own diagram; the model's diagram was the wrong one, and the twin had faithfully mirrored the intent instead of the code. The same held for the MLP half.

The proof. Feeding the dumped weights through the block with Bend's own wiring — h = ln(x); r = h + att(h); h2 = ln(r); y = h2 + mlp(h2) — reproduces Bend's blk0 to 7.5e-08 (-0.0620425 -0.1111432 -0.0104476 -0.1073963 0.0307670 0.0735757). The pre-LN wiring is 0.64 away. That is the whole issue, measured.

Why it matters beyond the gate. The stream was re-normalized twice per block, so the block had no identity path: every half threw away the incoming residual and kept only its norm. That is not the architecture the torch probe measured 0.809 with, and it is not trainable to the same standard. The 3,000-step run in flight at the time of the fix is the old wiring; it is reported as the "normalized residual" baseline and not as M1.

Fix. AttBlk is Residual(x, Seq(ln, att)) (AttIn), and Mlp is Residual(x, Seq(ln, mlp)) (MlpIn). The parameter order is unchanged — the norm's g and b still come before the layer's weights — so Layer.count stays 100,736, Layer.dump keeps its shape and twin.from_flat is untouched.

Component. Ours: reference/probe_vit_model.bend.

Verified by. reference/probe_vit_frozen.bend's ln0/att0/blk0/res0 lines against the twin, and reference/vit_gate.py after the fix: eps maxabs 1.19e-07 (tol 1e-05), one ancestral step 4.77e-07 (tol 1e-05), the 16-step DDIM cloud 6.38e-06 (tol 1e-04), and eta = 0 exactly 0. The forward gate went from 0.343 to one ulp of float32.

ISSUE-011 logged 2026-10-28 20:43 UTC

BendTorch's only attention is causal, so the ViT attends one way (BendTorch, high) — UPSTREAM (filed #17)

What. bendtorch@1.0.1.0's Attention has no mask argument, and its forward is causal by construction: for query position i, qrow builds scores against keys 0..i only (drow(i, ...), i + 1 dots) and prow softmaxes exactly those i + 1, leaving the rest of the t x t block at zero. The three trailing arguments of the layer are erased *shape* facts (n == b*t, w == kv*g*hd, kvw == kv*hd), not masks. Found 2026-10-28, 20:05.

How it showed up. The twin's bidirectional attention missed Bend's att0; masking the upper triangle reproduces it exactly (0.07157408 -0.09983167 0.03612092 ... both sides). Causality is not a tolerance here: it is the same numbers or not.

Why it matters. For a language model a causal mask is the point. For a denoiser it is a handicap with no reason: the 16 patch tokens are exchangeable, and token 0 can see only itself, token 15 sees all. It also means the ViT measured in torch (0.809 legibility, bidirectional) is *not* the architecture Bend can express, so the three-arm harness must run its torch arm with the same causal mask to be "the same architecture", and the numbers in docs/AUDIT.md from the bidirectional model are labelled as such.

Component. BendTorch (upstream); MLBend works around it.

Filed as nuxyel/BendTorch#17 (2026-10-29), with the signature (both lanes), the shape facts, and the twin evidence. The issue states plainly that this is an *expressiveness* gap, not an accuracy bug.

What the mask costs, measured (3000 steps, the same 16-token ViT, one head, width 64, the only difference the mask):

maskDDIM 16ancestral 64
causal (what BendTorch can express)0.7860.779
full (upper triangle masked)0.7540.774

So on this task causality costs nothing measurable — the earlier assumption that it was a handicap is not supported by these runs. The reason to want a mask is that a ViT encoder, a UNet's mid-block attention and cross-attention cannot be written at all without one.

ISSUE-013 fixed logged 2026-10-28 22:32 UTC

the "deterministic minibatch" was the same 64 digits every step (ours, high)

What. step_in built each step's batch with take_go(4096n, x0, Nil{}) — the first 64 of the 1024 digits, on every one of the 3000 steps. The torch arm of the comparison draws a fresh 64 from all 1024 per step, so the two arms were not training on the same task: Bend's was 16x smaller.

How it showed up. The loss curve, not the code: Bend reached 0.00546 at step 275, where torch's arm — same architecture, same lr, same batch — sits at 0.0608 after 3000 steps. A 100k-parameter model on 64 fixed images can memorize them; ten times lower loss than the arm that sees all 1024 is what memorizing looks like. Measured 2026-10-28, 22:35.

Why. The milestone asked for "a deterministic minibatch of 64 digits (the example is full-batch today, which is 16x the token cost)" — deterministic was carried out as *static*, which is a different thing. The rotation is what was meant: reproducible, and every digit seen.

Fix. batch_of(s, x0) takes chunk s mod 16 of the sixteen 64-digit chunks (drop_go drops whole chunks, then take_go 4096), so the step's data is a function of the step and all 1024 digits are covered every 16 steps. The first loss is unchanged (step 0 is chunk 0, the same 64 digits the twin's first_loss uses), so the first-loss gate still compares like with like.

Component. Ours: examples/diffusion_vit/main.bend.

Verified by. The loss curve above, and the code path: step_in was called with the same x0 and ignored s for the batch.

ISSUE-014 logged 2026-10-29 14:19 UTC

"BendTorch has no CUDA path" (ours, high) — WITHDRAWN, the claim was false

What. bendtorch@1.0.1.0 resolves to a package of eight files — main.bend, tensor.bend, dtype.bend, spec.bend, PROOF.bend, LAWS.bend, package.bend, LICENSE — and no torch/gpu.bend. Every layer is written over Array<F32> and runs on the CPU. The source says so itself where it matters: the erf comment reads "the C and JavaScript programs give the same bits (CUDA is not checked yet)". Found 2026-10-29, 14:20.

How it showed up. The machine has an RTX 5060 and CUDA 13.4; a 3000-step run of the 100,736-parameter ViT takes ~90 minutes on 28 cores, while the same architecture in PyTorch on the card is seconds. The reference arms can use the GPU (torch 2.14.1+cu130 installed into reference/.venv-lucidrains, which had a CPU-only wheel); the Bend arm cannot, at any model size.

Why it matters. The DoD's CIFAR-10 arm asks for "the compute decision (CPU budget against the CUDA lane, whose GPU layers must be verified for attention first)". There is no CUDA lane to verify: the layers are not device-abstracted at all, so Attention, LayerNorm and LinearNB have no GPU mirror to check. M5 stays a CPU-budget decision (10+ h) for Bend, while the comparison's torch arms move to the card.

Correction (2026-10-29, the same day). The core package is the law-clean CPU lane by design; the lanes are separate hub packages, published by scripts/package.sh: bendtorch-fast@1.1.1.0 (0x7185f584…), bendtorch-gpu@1.1.1.0 (0x3c22148d676c2988827dae103c18f499) and bendtorch-load@1.1.1.0 (0x0b2d5749…). torch/gpu.bend at v1.0.1 and v1.1.1 exports LinearF, LinearFirstF, LinearNBF, Residual and the same Attention. This ledger entry read the core's file list as the whole story and was wrong; BendTorch#12 was retracted by us for the same reason.

What is actually true, and ours. 1. We pin bendtorch@1.0.1.0 and import only main.bend — the CPU lane. The lane switch is a dependency change plus Linear -> LinearF, Attention unchanged (#13, closed, documents the naming gap). 2. This machine has no CUDA toolkit: bun bend2/main.ts reports "no CUDA toolkit (include/nvrtc.h under $CUDA_HOME, $CUDA_PATH, /usr/local/cuda or /opt/cuda), so ! runs on the CPU", and the GPU lane needs libnvrtc to compile its kernels. That is an environment item (toolkit headers, root), not a package bug.

Component. Ours: the dependency and the lane switch, and the machine's toolkit.

Next. Try bendtorch@1.1.1.0 + bendtorch-gpu@1.1.1.0 in a branch, swap the linear family, and measure the ViT on the card; install the CUDA toolkit for the nvrtc.h the RTC lane needs. Until then every Bend number is a CPU number, which is now written next to each run (device in the arm JSONs).

ISSUE-015 fixed logged 2026-10-29 14:42 UTC

the minibatch and the timestep cycle were locked, so each chunk saw 4 of 64 timesteps (ours, high)

What. The rotating minibatch took chunk s mod 16 of the sixteen 64-digit chunks, and the timestep is t = 1 + s mod 64. Since 16 divides 64, (t - 1) mod 16 = s mod 16 = chunk on every step: chunk c was only ever trained at t ∈ {c+1, c+17, c+33, c+49}. At sampling every chunk meets all 64 timesteps, so 60 of them had never been seen for that data. Found 2026-10-29, 14:45, from the numbers rather than the code.

How it showed up. The 3000-step run reached legibility 0.371 (min 0.249, max 0.593) with a final loss of 0.00133 — a *lower* loss than the torch arm's 0.0608 and *half* its legibility (0.786). A denoiser that fits its training pairs better and generalises worse is being asked, at sampling time, for something it never trained on; the correlation is the arithmetic above.

Fix. batch_of advances the chunk once per timestep sweep: chunk = (s div 64) mod 16. Sixty-four consecutive steps hold one chunk while t runs 1..64, so every (chunk, t) pair is covered every 1024 steps, and step 0 is still chunk 0 (loss_at(0) unchanged at 1.0069675). A fresh 3000-step run (m1-fix) measures the difference.

Component. Ours: examples/diffusion_vit/main.bend.

Verified by. The congruence above, and the loss/legibility pair (0.00133 / 0.371 against the torch arm's 0.0608 / 0.786).
