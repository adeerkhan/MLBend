ISSUE-001 fixed logged 2026-10-27 22:17 UTC

the digits denoiser learned nothing (ours, high)

What. examples/diffusion_digits/main.bend trained a 3-layer SiLU MLP for 3000 full-batch AdamW steps and ended at MSE 1.0258 (NumPy twin 1.0746). The target is unit-variance noise, so predicting nothing scores exactly 1.0: both arms were at or worse than the mean.

Why. value(i, j, seed) returned k*0.1 - 0.2 for k ∈ 0..6, i.e. a uniform init on [−0.2, +0.5] with mean +0.100. The first layer is 72 wide, so the layer adds 72 × 0.100 × 0.329 ≈ +2.4 to every pre-activation (measured: z1 mean +2.36, rms 2.43); SiLU is roughly linear there (h1 mean +2.16), and each following layer multiplies that common mode by fan_in × 0.100 = 6.4 — z2 +13.8, the output +88.4 rms. Against a target of rms 1.0 that is the measured first loss, 8062.6895 (the reference's is 0.963). Adam then had to undo a 10⁴× scale with lr 0.01, and 3000 steps were not enough — it landed on the mean predictor and stayed.

How it showed up. The loss dropped 8062.7 → ~18 by step 98 and then sat between 0.99 and 1.06 for the remaining 2900 steps. Downstream, the sampled cloud was meaningless: sample_mean 1.2e17, sample_std 1.075e19, 0 of 4096 pixels inside [0,1].

Component. Ours: examples/diffusion_digits/main.bend, the weight initialiser. Not BendTorch, not the schedule — the NumPy twin plateaued at the same 1.0.

Verified by. Three independent measurements: 1. The failure itself: results/diffusion-digits/run.json, and the twin's numpy_answer(). 2. A controlled ablation of our exact MLP, optimizer and data (reference/digits_ablation.py), one factor at a time — the init was the only factor that mattered:

bend [-0.2, 0.5][0,1]bend3480.0362.201.3766
torch zero-mean[0,1]bend1.01380.32110.1455
torch zero-mean[−1,1]bend1.01330.36370.1755

(3000 steps, batch 128, lr 1e-3, identical data and seeds.) 3. The fix in the real example: first loss 1.0039 (the twin reads 1.003894329, delta 2.9e-08), final 0.0417 — see ISSUE-005 for the sampler that then had to be fixed too.

Fix. value() is now a 97-value hash on [−1, 1) with zero mean, scaled by 1/sqrt(fan_in) at the row builder — PyTorch's nn.Linear init made deterministic, which is what the reference uses. The NumPy twin mirrors it.

ISSUE-002 logged 2026-10-27 22:17 UTC

the two "linear schedules" are not the same schedule (ours, medium) — DECIDED (documented)

What. mlbend-generative's lin_beta_at(t, 64) is 1e-4 + 0.2799·t/64: β = 0.0044734 … 0.28, ᾱ₆₄ = 4.085385e-05. The reference's linear_beta_schedule(64) is linspace(1000/T·1e-4, 1000/T·0.02, T): β = 0.0015625 … 0.3125, ᾱ₆₄ = 1.223548e-05. Worst per-step relative gap: 65.07%.

Why. Two conventions both called "the linear schedule": DDPM's 1000-step schedule rescaled by 1000/T, and a directly parameterised 0 → 0.28 ramp. Neither is wrong; they are not interchangeable.

How it showed up. Only when the two were printed side by side (reference/lucidrains_compare.py, "the schedules"). No gate could see it, because each arm was compared with its own mirror.

Component. Ours (mlbend-generative); the reference defines the other convention.

Verified by. The script above, and the library's own diffusion.alphas_cumprod[-1] printed in the same run.

Status. Decided: documented, not changed. The ablation says it does not explain ISSUE-001 (0.1755 vs 0.1455), and the 3,000-step runs reach the same order either way. Our schedule is the 1e-4 + 0.2799·t/T ramp, which is what mlbend-generative describes in lin_beta_at; the reference's is the 1000-step schedule rescaled by 1000/T. They are different schedules, not two spellings of one, and the difference is now written down in NOTES (2026-10-28) rather than left as an unexplained divergence.

ISSUE-003 fixed logged 2026-10-27 22:17 UTC

the digits grid draws each cell 1.7× too wide (ours, medium)

What. grid_svg in reference/test_diffusion_digits.py computes a pixel's x as ox + c*cell//8*8//8 + c*(cell/8). The stray first term is floor(1.75·c), so with cell = 14 the last sub-column starts 24.25 px into a 14 px cell. The y offset has no such term.

Why. A leftover from an earlier layout; it duplicates part of the intended c*(cell/8).

How it showed up. Reading the generated SVG while checking the digits plot: the data cells smear into their neighbours.

Component. Ours (reference/test_diffusion_digits.py).

Verified by. The code, and a re-render keeping only c*(cell/8) (reference/lucidrains_compare.py:grid_svg), which aligns.

Status. Fixed: the offset is ox + c*(cell/8); the committed grid.svg and the example's plot.svg were regenerated with it.

ISSUE-005 fixed logged 2026-10-27 22:17 UTC

the sampler has no x̂₀ clamp, so the cloud explodes (ours, high)

What. After ISSUE-001 the digits model learned (final loss 0.0417, better than the reference's 0.078) but its sampled cloud was std 35.8 with mean −1.39, against the data's mean 0.3076 / std 0.3767 — and 0 of 4096 pixels inside [0,1].

Why. The reverse step formed its mean directly from the raw ε̂. The vendored reference reconstructs the start and clamps it to the data range before forming the posterior mean (p_mean_variance with clip_denoised=True, and ddim_sample with clip_x_start=True, re-deriving ε̂ from the clamped start). Without that clamp one bad prediction compounds over 64 steps; with it the sample cannot leave the data range.

How it showed up. The training loss was healthy while the samples were not, which is what pointed away from the trainer and at the sampler.

Component. Ours: examples/diffusion_digits/main.bend (and its twin).

Verified by. A controlled probe with one trained MLP, sampling it four ways (reference/digits_sample_probe.py, same weights, same schedule, same seeds):

samplerclampmeanstdin [0,1]
DDPMno−2.507432.7934123/4096
DDPMyes+0.29600.30054096/4096
DDIMno−0.797817.7724194/4096
DDIMyes+0.38990.36144096/4096

(data: mean 0.3076, std 0.3767)

Fix. Both steps now reconstruct x0 = (x − √(1−ᾱ)·ε̂)/√ᾱ, clamp it to [0,1], and form the mean from it; DDIM re-derives ε̂ from the clamped start (the reference's rederive_pred_noise). The twin mirrors both.

ISSUE-006 fixed logged 2026-10-27 22:17 UTC

both NumPy twins took the SiLU derivative at the wrong point (ours, high)

What. The two NumPy twins (reference/test_diffusion_digits.py, reference/test_diffusion.py) computed the SiLU backward as sigmoid(h) * (1 + h*(1 - sigmoid(h))) where h is the activated output. BendTorch's silu_d(dy, x) takes x = the pre-activation; the derivative is s(z)*(1 + z*(1-s(z))), s = sigmoid(z). The twins kept only h = silu(z), so their gradient was wrong wherever z ≠ silu(z).

Why. The forward was written first (h = silu(z)), and the backward was then written against h, which is the value in scope — the pre-activation was never stored.

How it showed up. It could not show up until ISSUE-001 was fixed: while both arms sat at the mean predictor the gradients are ~0, so a wrong gradient and a right one agree. After the init fix, from an identical first loss (1.0038943 vs 1.003894329, delta 6e-9) Bend reached 0.044 in 3000 steps and the twin stayed at 1.031; its sampled cloud (0.5018/0.3982) only looked sane because of the ISSUE-005 clamp.

Component. Ours: the two twins' training loops. Not Bend, not BendTorch — Bend's layer was right, which is exactly why the arms diverged.

Verified by. The identical first loss with divergent finals (above); the same bug in both twins, found by reading the backward after the divergence; and BendTorch's own silu_d in .bend/env/.../main.bend, which takes the pre-activation as x.

Fix. Both twins now keep z1, z2 and evaluate the derivative there. The gated first loss is unchanged (one step, no update), which is why this was invisible to make check.

  • H3 — the data range ([0,1] vs [−1,1]) is the cause. Dismissed by the ablation: reference init on [0,1] scored 0.1455, on [−1,1] 0.1755 — the same order, and worse centred. Kept as a fidelity option only.
  • H4 — the time embedding as 8 concatenated input features is the cause. Not supported: the ablation kept that layout and learned fine once the init was fixed. The reference injects the embedding as per-block scale/shift; a fidelity question for the model, not the cause of the failure.
  • Nondeterminism of Bend training. Two runs of the same binary (before these fixes) were bit-identical: final loss 1.0258427, same sample line. The old report card's different numbers came from a pre-2f2631b code state.
ISSUE-007 upstream logged 2026-10-27 23:55 UTC

bend file.bend overflows its stack on a 32k-element list (bendlang/bend, high)

What. The plain run path dies with bend: memory fault (machine stack overflow?), exit 1, no source location, on a twelve-line program:

import Base
import mlbend-generative@0.1.1.0/main.bend as G

def main() -> IO(Unit):
  xs = G.noise_of(7n, 32768n)
  do IO<Unit>:
    IO.print(Nat.show(List.length(&2, F32, xs)))
    return Unit{}

Where the line is. 4096, 8192 and 16384 run and print their count; 32768 and 65536 crash. Same on Bend 2.0.38 and 2.0.39. The same file built with bend … -o prog and then run prints 65536 on both versions.

Why. The run path's stack is fixed, and each list element is built by a Box–Muller call whose frame is large enough that ~32k of them do not fit; the native build has no such limit (the guide: the compiled model has no C stack; each def is a segment of a flat state machine). It is frame size, not depth alone: a 65536-element Nat list summed by recursion on the same path is fine.

How it showed up. Running examples/diffusion_digits/main.bend the documented way. --check-only passes and -o runs the full 3000-step training, so it looked like a fault in the program; it is not — it is the run path, and the message names no file and no line.

Component. bendlang/bend, the bend <file.bend> (check, then run) path.

Verified by. The program above on both versions, the 16384/32768 threshold, the -o contrast at 65536, and the Nat-list contrast. Filed as bendlang/bend#1894.

ISSUE-009 fixed logged 2026-10-28 19:40 UTC

the Bend ViT omitted the final LayerNorm before the head (ours, medium)

What. The port's model was embed -> 3 blocks -> Linear(64,4), while the torch spec that measured 0.809 is embed -> 3 blocks -> LayerNorm -> Linear(64,4). The omission was invisible in the type check and in a zero-parameter forward.

Why. The head was written from the "one head per patch" line of the spec and the normaliser in front of it was dropped when the Bend Head layer was built as a bare LinearNB.

How it showed up. Writing the NumPy twin (reference/twin_vit.py) forced the forward to be spelled out, and the spec's self.head(self.ln(x)) had no counterpart in the Bend model. The parameter count says it plainly: 100,608 without the norm, 100,736 with it.

Component. Ours: reference/probe_vit_model.bend.

Verified by. The torch probe's forward (self.ln = nn.LayerNorm(DIM) then self.head(...)) against the Bend Head, and the parameter count.

Fix. HeadN() is LayerNorm -> Head, and Model() chains it. The 3,000-step run that was in flight when this was found reflects the *old* model; the next run measures the corrected one.

Checked and dismissed logged 2026-10-27 22:17 UTC
  • accelerate "no matching distribution" on Python 3.14. pip said from versions: none, which looked like a hard incompatibility. PyPI's metadata says requires_python >=3.10.0, and a retry installed accelerate 1.15.0 on 3.14 unchanged — the failure was a transient index interruption, not the library.
  • The forward dot product's four partial sums (7 ulps from NumPy). I measured Bend's eps 5.34e-05 (7 ulps at magnitude 95) from the twin's, traced it to dotr in bend-ml-tensor-array, and intended to file it. On inspection it is not a defect: the four accumulators are documented at the top of dotr as the design, and Mat.matmul_precise — the "precise product for long dots" — only re-blocks sums longer than 1024, so a 72-term dot is four-accumulator in both variants. A few ulps is what any two summation orders give; it is a measurement to set tolerances with (it is why the sampler gate shares the model output), not a bug. Not filed.
ISSUE-008 upstream logged 2026-10-27 23:55 UTC

no convolution and no group norm, so no UNet (BendTorch, high)

What. BendTorch exports no Conv2d, no GroupNorm and no BatchNorm (constructor list in the issue). A UNet — Conv2d + GroupNorm + attention, the reference's denoiser — cannot be expressed, so image diffusion has no natural model.

Why it matters, measured. Same data, task, schedule and 3000 steps; the samples are scored by the best absolute correlation with any training digit (random images: 0.326):

denoiserparamsnearest-digit \corr\
the reference's UNet825k0.926 (min 0.813)
our dense 72→64→64→64 MLP13k0.408 (min 0.239)

The MLP has the *lower* training loss (0.042 against 0.078) and still generates mush: dense layers over a flattened digit carry no spatial structure. That is the gap.

Component. BendTorch (layers). Not a defect — an absent capability.

Status. Filed as nuxyel/BendTorch#15. Interim route: a ViT-style denoiser from Attention + LayerNorm, which do exist; Conv2d later. Measured (reference/digits_vit_probe.py): 16 tokens of 2×2 patches, dim 64, 4 heads, 3 blocks, 103k parameters, 3000 steps — nearest-digit |corr| 0.798 (min 0.676), cloud 0.260/0.299, 4096/4096 in [0,1]. The route works; the port is adeerkhan/MLBend#16.
